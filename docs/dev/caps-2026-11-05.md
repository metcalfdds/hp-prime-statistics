# Reasonable limits on everything a student types (round 10, 5 November 2026, afternoon)

*Roger, 5 November 2026, after the random-use test typed a df of 999,999 into StatDist: "Don't do ridiculous things on a calculator like that 999,999 df - that's ludicrous -- cap everything at reasonable values". On the first list of limits: "sd not up to 1,000,000 --try like 1,000 ... reasonable values". And: "and note those limits in the write ups too". This replaces round 6's rule that values in the data's own units stay open ([caps-2026-11-02.md](caps-2026-11-02.md)).*

## The limits

| What a student types | The range |
|---|---|
| A mean, an x value, a bound or a cut-off | −10,000 to 10,000 |
| An SD or another scale | above 0, at most 1,000 |
| Degrees of freedom | above 0, at most 1,000 |
| A rate (the Poisson λ and the like) | above 0, at most 1,000 |
| StatDist's binomial n, a shape parameter | at most 1,000 |
| A sample size typed into a form | at most 10,000 (PowerViz: 2 to 5,000, as before) |
| A count in a table (ChiTable, EpiStats, BayesTree) | at most 100,000 |
| α | 0.001 to 0.2 (round 6) |
| A random seed (BurrLab, ResampLab) | 0 to 9,999 |

The count and seed limits were Claude's picks, told to Roger with the reasons: a real survey table holds a few thousand people a cell, so 1,000 would be too few; a seed only has to repeat a run. Left as they were: ErrorRate's database size, 1 to 100,000,000, because national DNA databases hold tens of millions of profiles; StatDist's tighter shape ranges (the Weibull's k 0.05 to 100 and so on); TrendTest's level total, at most 99,999; AgreeLab's tile, at most 99,999. Data loaded into the D lists or a matrix are not limited.

Roger, 5 November 2026: "note that the calculator has been tested to handle much larger values, but the entries are capped at reasonable values for processing speed considerations." The random-use and big-value tests type numbers up to 1E100, and the calculator's arithmetic goes to about 1E499; the limits are for speed and sense, not because the Prime cannot hold the numbers.

The hint line under each field gives its range, and a value outside it is refused with a sentence that gives the range again; nothing changes. ExhibitLab's last clamps (c, the claim, the negatives allowed and the prior, each moved to the nearest end with a sentence) are refusals now.

## The probe

`tools/caps_probe_v1.py`, 40 runs a program, 12 s each, on the 28 programs the PC harness runs (not CentralLimit, an app, or StatDist, done by hand), before and after:

- before (round 9 files): 202 fields in 28 programs; 97 flagged
- after (round 10 files): 192 fields in 28 programs; 24 flagged

The 24 flags after are all of four kinds: a refusal whose sentence the probe does not read as one ("M": BayesTree, DNAMixLR, EpiStats); a check that runs after the last of several forms, so the form itself looks like an acceptance (TrendTest's scores and cases, TTestLab's numbers, checked in tt_Calc: a mean of 1,000,000 gives "A mean must be from -10,000 to 10,000 and an SD at most 1,000"); StudRange's MSE at its limit, 1,000,000; and the two left open on purpose (ErrorRate's database, SurvLab's interval width, which the 100-interval rule bounds).

## How to rerun

`python3 tools/caps_probe_v1.py programs/<file>.txt "<Main>()" --runs 40 --seconds 12 --lists --out <Name>.json`, then `python3 tools/summarize_probe_v1.py <folder>`.
