# AnovaLab beta 1i: changes (15 November 2026)

Round 16. Built from beta 1h by exact replacements (`verify/round16/make_anovalab_1i.py`; a replacement that does not match exactly once stops the build). 4,738 lines (beta 1h: 3,881); 166 routines (139), of which 27 are new and 25 changed (`verify/round16/linemap_r16.py` lists them).

## Why

Three reasons, in the order they came up.

1. **Dunnett's test: every group against one control.** Roger, 15 November 2026, had a book of tables checked against fresh calculations (Zwillinger & Kokoska, 2000; the report is `claude/CRC_table_check_2026-11-15.md` in the Project). Its two-sided Dunnett table (pp. 297 and 298) prints the values of Dunnett's 1955 paper, which are too large for three or more treatments: 303 of 384 cells (at 5 df and 5 %, 3.39 for 3.29 with three treatments and 4.49 for 3.97 with nine). Dunnett published new tables in 1964. The report's first suggestion for our programs was "Dunnett's comparisons with a control, computed exactly, in AnovaLab. The printed two-sided table is wrong for three or more treatments, so a program that integrates is better than the table." Of the six suggestions Roger said: "Add all please." A program also does what no table does: it takes the data's own group sizes, equal or not, and gives each comparison a p.
2. **Numbers cut by their places.** Found by this version's layout check, run with data in the thousands and beyond. `TEXTOUT_P` draws as much of a text as its width argument holds and drops the rest without a sign. So beta 1h cut any number wider than its place: a sum of squares of 2,467,448,033 in the ANOVA table's 62 pixels (data in the thousands are enough), a mean in the millions on the Checks page, a difference and its interval on the Pairs page, the y numbers of the three plots from 100,000 up, and two lines of the variances page. A number cut short is a wrong number that looks right. Old code.
3. **The titles of the boxes that ask before a long computation.** Found on the Virtual Calculator in this round: a `CHOOSE` title shows 31 characters and no more, so the first build's "Dunnett d crit: about 14-41 s on a G1" stood as "Dunnett d crit: about 14–41 s o". Beta 1h's "Tukey q crit: about 14-41 s on a G1" has been cut the same way since it was written.

**What was read.** The HP-41C Users' Library Solutions book *Applied Statistics I*, program STAT.08: after an ANOVA it gives each group's t against the control, and the user compares it with Dunnett's printed table (it cites Dunnett, 1964); no probability is computed there. It was read to see what it computes, and none of its code is here. The library in `D:\HP-Prime` holds no other program with Dunnett's test (searched 15 November 2026). The engine is written from the formulas below (Dunnett, 1955, 1964).

**The arithmetic.** For group g against the control c, t_g = (mean_g − mean_c) / √(MSW (1/n_g + 1/n_c)). Given the control's mean and the pooled s, the k − 1 statistics are independent. With z the control's standardized mean and S = s/σ:

P(every |t_g| ≤ c) = ∫ f_S(s) G(c s) ds, G(y) = ∫ φ(z) ∏_g [Φ(b_g z + y a_g) − Φ(b_g z − y a_g)] dz, b_g = √(n_g/n_c), a_g = √(1 + b_g²).

G depends on the group sizes only. It is tabulated once for a control, at y = 0, 0.1, …, 7 (71 values), each by Simpson's rule over z, and read by four-point cubic interpolation; groups of one size share a factor. The upper tail P(max |t| > c) is summed as it stands, never as 1 minus a lower tail, over 64 Simpson intervals. d crit is the c whose tail is α, by the regula falsi the Tukey engine uses (8 to 22 tails, 12 as a rule). The adjusted p of a group is the tail at its own |t|: the chance that the largest |t| of the family is this big when no group differs from the control.

**Accuracy.** Against a fine Gauss–Legendre reference, over 18 sets of group sizes (2 to 12 groups, equal and unequal, a control from 1/100 of the values up) and df from 3 to 2,990: the p is within 0.032 % of its value wherever it is 0.0001 or more (the header and help page 5 promise 0.05 %; a smaller p prints as "< 0.0001"), and d crit within 0.0039 % for α from 0.2 to 0.001 (promised: 0.005 %). With one group beside the control the test is the two-sided t-test, and d crit is t crit. Fifteen of Dunnett's 1964 two-sided 5 % points are reproduced to the places printed. SciPy's `stats.dunnett` gives the same 18 t statistics on the three presets, and its p-values, which come from a randomized integration good to about three decimals, are within 0.0001 of ours.

## Unresolved

- Nothing has run on a G1 or a G2, and the estimates have not been held against a stopwatch.
- **The book names menu items by number.** Six items of the one-way menu moved down one (the table above). Draft 37 must be searched for them.
- The two questions come one after the other, and the second box opens over the Program Catalog's list, not over AnovaLab's page: the calculator repaints its own view when a box closes, and the program has not drawn again yet. Every program with two boxes in a row does the same; it is odd to look at and does no harm.
- The test is two-sided only. A one-sided Dunnett test (is any treatment better than the control?) would need its own integral; the CRC book's one-sided tables are right, for those who need them.
- With data of a few ten-millionths the older pages still print some values at fixed decimals; Dunnett's pages and the fitted places do not. Such data are not what the program is for.
- On the Virtual Calculator some key presses did nothing on pages that wait in the fleet's `GETKEY` and `MOUSE` loop; of 30 Down presses on Dunnett's plot, 26 moved the pick (`verify/round16/keyprobe_r16.txt`).
