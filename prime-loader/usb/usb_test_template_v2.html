HP Prime USB test 2

This page tries whether a web page can put a program on a real HP Prime through its USB cable, with no Connectivity Kit, and read it back to see that it arrived whole.

This is the second test. The first ran on a G2 and a G1 on 17 November 2026. It found that a program travels and comes back the same when it is sent not compressed, up to 80 reports of the G2; that a compressed program was not kept; and then the page stood still. This one asks what that left open, and it cannot stand still: every wait in it has an end.

The page is for the book's author, or for an instructor with a calculator to spare. It is not for students: they should use the HP Prime Loader.

This browser cannot talk to a USB device

Open this page in Microsoft Edge or Google Chrome on the Windows computer the calculator is plugged into. Firefox and Safari do not let a web page reach a USB device, and neither does a phone.

1Before you start

  1. Back the calculator up first, if it holds anything you would miss. HP's Connectivity Kit can make the backup.

    The test writes four test programs of its own and deletes them again. But a calculator that freezes may need its reset hole, which can cost what is in its memory.

  2. Close HP's Connectivity Kit.

    Windows gives a USB device to one program at a time. While the Kit is open, this page cannot reach the calculator.

  3. Turn the calculator on, leave it at its Home screen, and plug it into this computer with its USB cable.
  4. Just before you click Run, press the Home key on the calculator.

    A calculator switches itself off after about five minutes without a key. The test takes two or three, and half way through it asks you to press a key once more.

2Connect

The browser opens a small window that lists the calculator. Click its name, then click Connect. The browser asks every time, so that no page can reach a device behind your back.

3Run the test

The test tries four routes, each with a program of a few lines first. A route that works climbs toward the size of the book's largest program (EpiStats, 289,813 characters). It takes two or three minutes; do not unplug the calculator while it runs.

On the calculator it writes four programs at most, named ZZUSBC, ZZUSBA, ZZUSBE and ZZUSBD, and deletes them at the end. It reads, changes and deletes nothing else.

4Send the log

    
The log is what tells us what to build next. It holds what was sent and what came back, in numbers; it does not hold the calculator's serial number, or anything from the calculator but the test programs and how its answers begin. Save it and attach the file to your chat with Claude, whatever the test found, failures most of all.
