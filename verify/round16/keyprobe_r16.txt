KeyProbe beta 1 on the HP Prime Virtual Calculator 2.4.2 (build 2026-09-09), run under Wine 11.21 in the cloud
workspace, 15 Nov 2026 (times are U.S. Central).  The program is diagnostics/KeyProbe_beta1.txt; it was Checked there
at 22:26 ("No errors in the program").

WHY.  While the round's programs were driven with xdotool, a key press now and then did nothing: Esc on a help page,
Down on Dunnett's plot, Enter on a help page.  The presses were clicks on the skin's keys, held 0.35 s, sent seconds
apart on pages that had long finished drawing.  On AnovaLab's Dunnett plot the Down key was pressed 30 times from a
tapped first row, 5 times at each of six lengths from 0.05 s to 0.8 s: 26 presses moved the pick one row, 4 moved
nothing (one each at 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.35 s), and none moved it two rows.

AT HOME, nothing is lost: the 1 key pressed 30 times (held 0.25 s, 0.4 s apart) put 30 digits on the entry line (22:19).
So the emulator and xdotool deliver every press, and the loss is inside a running program.

THE PROBE.  One of six ways to wait, then the Down key pressed n times by a click on the skin, 0.45 s between presses,
and the count the program shows ("keys seen") read from the screen.

    way to wait                                     first pass     second pass    third pass
                                                    40 presses     60 presses     60 presses
                                                    held 0.2 s     held 0.1 s     held 0.2 s
                                                    22:28-22:32    22:38-22:42    22:44-22:48
                                                    (other jobs    (other jobs    (nothing else
                                                    running)       running)       running)
    1  GETKEY alone                                     24             38             41
    2  GETKEY, then MOUSE  (the fleet's loop)           33             48             52
    3  MOUSE, then GETKEY                               31              -              -
    4  WAIT(-1)                                         40             60             60
    5  drain, GETKEY and MOUSE, drain (the idiom)       37             52             50
    6  GETKEY, MOUSE, GETKEY again                      34              -              -

    Also in the second pass: GETKEY alone with the key held 0.35 s saw 34 of 60.  (That pass was stopped there.)

    All passes together:  GETKEY alone 137 of 220 (62 %);  GETKEY then MOUSE 133 of 160 (83 %);  the fleet's idiom 139
    of 160 (87 %);  WAIT(-1) 160 of 160.

WHAT IT SHOWS
  - A loop that polls GETKEY loses key presses on this calculator: about four in ten when it does nothing else, about
    one in seven when each pass also reads MOUSE.  The order of the two calls makes no difference that 40 presses can
    show, and a second GETKEY after MOUSE does not help.
  - WAIT(-1) lost none of 160.
  - The length of the press does not matter between 0.05 s and 0.35 s.  What the machine is doing besides does not
    matter either (the third pass ran alone).
  - The drains are not the cause: the bare loop loses more than the idiom with its drains.

WHAT IT DOES NOT SHOW
  - Whether a G1 or a G2 loses presses.  The Virtual Calculator runs the calculators' firmware built for a PC, and the
    loss may belong to that build's keyboard.  Roger's G1 notes of 20 Oct 2026 say a light tap on a soft key registers
    and a tap followed by a key works; nobody has counted presses there.  The count takes two minutes: run KeyProbe,
    choose 2, press Down 40 times at an even pace, read "keys seen", press Esc; then the same with 1 and with 4.
  - Why.  One reading that fits: GETKEY looks at a one-key slot that the system's own event handling empties too, so a
    tight loop sometimes loses the race, and a loop that spends time in MOUSE loses it less often; WAIT(-1) takes the
    event from the queue itself.  That is a guess from the counts, not something read in HP's documents.

WHAT FOLLOWS FOR THE FLEET
  Nothing was changed in this round.  If the calculators lose presses too, the cure is a wait built on WAIT(-1) in
  place of the GETKEY + MOUSE loop, with the three things already known about it (Lessons Learned s7): a touch
  arrives as a list whose size must be tested, a tap on the soft keys arrives only as type 3, and WAIT(-1) gives up
  after about a minute and must be called again.  All 31 programs wait in the same loop, so it would be a round of
  its own, tried first on one program on a G1.
