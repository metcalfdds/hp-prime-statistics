HP Prime Data Loader

Paste columns from Excel, SPSS or a CSV file and get the calculator's own matrix file. The DataLoad program on the Prime then puts each column, or each group's scores, into the Statistics lists that TTestLab, AnovaLab, ScatterLab and the other programs read.

1Paste your data

In Excel or SPSS, select your cells, with the column names in the first row if you have them, and copy them (Ctrl+C). Then click in the box below and paste (Ctrl+V). Put one variable in each column and one case in each row. Empty cells, and cells that say NA or ., become blanks.

2Check the columns

    UseOn the PrimeColumnKind ValuesBlanksRange or codesFirst values
    Not right?

    Look at the table before you go on: each column you want should be there under its own name, with as many values as you expect and a sensible range. Untick a column to leave it out.

    Text columns (such as male / female) are numbered 1, 2, 3 … in alphabetical order, as the codes column shows: the calculator holds numbers only. Write the codes down; DataLoad puts the smallest code's scores in D1.

    The Virtual Calculator (Windows)

    1. Open the HP Prime Loader. This link opens it ready for a data file.
    2. In its step 2, click Add a data file you were given and choose M1.hpmat from your Downloads folder.
    3. Close the Virtual Calculator, then click Load. The Loader checks the file, puts it where the calculator looks for it, and makes sure the DataLoad program is on the calculator. Your other programs stay.
    4. Start the calculator and open the Statistics 1Var app once (press Apps). Then press Shift, then Program, tap DataLoad, tap Run, and choose Columns or Scores by group.
    5. If DataLoad says lists after the new ones hold data from before, choose clear: AnovaLab, RankLab and AgreeLab read every list that holds data as one more group (or rater).
    Or copy the file yourself
    1. Close the Virtual Calculator (its Calculator menu, Exit). It reads its folder only when it starts, and it writes over the folder when it closes.
    2. Copy M1.hpmat into Documents\HP Prime\Calculators\Prime and replace the file that is there. The name must be exactly M1.hpmat: if your browser called a second download M1 (1).hpmat, rename it first.
    3. Start the calculator and go on from step 4 above. DataLoad must already be on the calculator.

    A real HP Prime (G1 or G2)

    1. Connect the calculator to the HP Connectivity Kit.
    2. Drag M1.hpmat onto the calculator in the Kit, or open its matrix M1 there and paste the columns.
    3. Run DataLoad on the calculator.

    Not yet tested on a G1 or G2. Large data sets load slowly over the Kit; send one matrix at a time.

    Part of hp-prime-statistics, Roger Metcalf's teaching programs for the HP Prime. Written with AI assistance (Anthropic Claude). Free for educational and personal use. A teaching aid: not validated for forensic, clinical or legal use.

    Everything happens in this page; nothing you paste leaves your computer. Numbers are read from their digits and rounded to the Prime's 12 significant digits, half up. The file format is the one hp-prime-kit (JordiRigau/hp-prime-kit, MIT) documents. Blank cells are written as 9.99999999999E499, the Prime's largest real, which DataLoad leaves out. A currency sign or a percent sign is dropped from a number, and labels that differ only in capitals or spacing are one label; step 2 says so whenever it happens.

    Tested on the Virtual Calculator 2.4.2 (build 2026-09-09), 31 Oct 2026: the example's 432 rows × 5 columns arrived whole, DataLoad put them in D1–D5 and the weeks by financial aid in D1 and D2, and TTestLab read those two lists (separate-variances t(421.19) = −1.68, p = 0.094).

    Example data: Rossi, Berk & Lenihan (1980), Money, Work, and Crime: 432 men released from Maryland state prisons, followed 52 weeks (week of first re-arrest, arrest 1/0, financial aid 1/0, age, prior convictions), via the R package carData.
